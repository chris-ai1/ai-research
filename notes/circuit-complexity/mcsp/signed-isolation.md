# Isolating a pattern after signed identifications

Let $A_s$ be the $N=2^n$-bit truth tables of inner circuit size at most $s$ over the full binary basis. Put $T=s\log_2 s$, and assume $n=o(s)$ and $T^3=o(N)$. After $t$ signed identifications of independent truth-table bits, exact MCSP can still isolate any prescribed pattern on $k$ remaining variables whenever $t+k\le(1-\eta)T$ for fixed $\eta>0$ and sufficiently large $n$. The bound charges one bit per independent identification, even if all the pairs use different positions.

More explicitly, the $t$ equations form a forest on original positions, with an equation $x_i\oplus x_j=b_{ij}$ on each edge. Treat isolated positions as singleton components. Each component has one free representative. Choose any $k$ distinct representatives and their values. There is a fixing of all other representatives for which that pattern is the unique accepted assignment. Conversely, for a fixed forest and choice of representatives, realizing every forest labeling and every $k$-bit target requires at least $2^{t+k}$ tables. The rooted circuit census thus gives the matching upper bound $t+k\le(1+o(1))T$ for a universal guarantee. [Circuit census](../../../results/circuit-complexity/mcsp/papers/deterministic/main.tex)

## Isolation along a space of allowed changes

Here is a more general statement. Work over $\mathbb F_2$. Fix a constant $C$ and a set $S$ of at most $CT$ truth-table addresses. Suppose $P$ has rank $t$ on the bits of $S$, and let $V\subseteq\ker P$ be a $k$-dimensional space of changes on $S$. Extend these changes by zero outside $S$. Let $D$ be a $k$-row matrix on $S$ whose restriction to $V$ is a bijection onto $\mathbb F_2^k$. For any $b,d$ and $t+k\le(1-\eta)T$, there exists $f\in A_s$ such that

$$
P f|_S=b,\qquad D f|_S=d,\qquad (f+V)\cap A_s=\{f\}.
$$

For the forest, take $S$ to contain all endpoints and the selected representatives. Its size is at most $2t+k$. Use $P$ for the edge parities and let $V$ be generated by toggling every bit of each selected component. Reading its representative gives the corresponding row of $D$. Fixing all other representatives gives precisely the cube $f+V$.

**A periodic witness for the prescribed values.** The stacked matrix $(P,D)$ has rank $t+k$. Any relation among its rows, evaluated on $V$, first forces all coefficients of the $D$ rows to vanish. The independence of the $P$ rows finishes the argument. Choose a linear address hash $M$ with $m=\lceil2\log_2(2(C+1)T)\rceil$ output bits, injective on $S$. A random matrix works with positive probability by a union bound over pairs. We have $m<n$ for large $n$ and the hash costs $o(s)$ by grouping its parity outputs when $s\le n^2$. [Hash cost](isolation.md)

The rows of $(P,D)$ supported on the hashed addresses retain their rank. Extend them on the $2^m$ hashed positions to rank $r=\lfloor(1-\eta)T\rfloor$ and choose any right-hand sides for the added rows. Here $m=O(\log r)$ and $m^{30}\le r\le 2^m$ for large $n$. The polynomial-rank interpolation theorem therefore gives a table $h$ on the shorter address with inner size at most $(1+o(1))r/\log_2 r$. The composite $h(Mx)$ realizes $b,d$ and has size $(1-\eta+o(1))s$. [Polynomial-rank affine interpolation](../../../results/circuit-complexity/mcsp/papers/affine/sparse-sharp.tex)

**Disjoint translates.** The sets $S+u$ for $u\in\ker M$ are pairwise disjoint, since the hash is injective on $S$. The periodic witness has the same prescribed values on each translated copy. If the claimed isolation fails, every accepted table with the prescribed values on $S$ has another accepted table obtained by adding a nonzero element of $V$. Address-translation invariance gives the same rule on each fresh copy, using the translated move space there. Starting from the periodic witness, choose any subset of the copies and perform one change on each chosen copy. Earlier changes preserve all entries on the fresh copies. The resulting accepted table differs from the starting table on exactly the chosen copies. This would give at least $2^{|\ker M|}$ accepted tables, while

$$
|\ker M|\ \ge\ N/2^m=\Omega_C(N/T^2)\ \gg\ T.
$$

The rooted census gives $\log_2|A_s|\le(1+o(1))T$, a contradiction. The counting argument is Samuel Schlesinger's disjoint-translate argument applied with the prescribed move space. [Original block argument](isolation.md) · [Lean counting lemma](formal/LocalIsolation.lean)

## A constraint on private circuit inputs

Let $r=\lfloor(1-\eta)T\rfloor$. After $t$ signed identifications, fixing any $b$ other free representatives leaves every remaining representative essential if $t+b+1\le r$. Apply the isolation result to the prescribed values of those $b$ bits and one additional bit. The isolated table is accepted and flipping the additional representative alone is rejected.

In a circuit for this restricted MCSP problem, suppose a representative $u$ feeds just one gate $G(u,H)$, and $G$ depends on both inputs and is nonlinear. Its partner $H$ has a value that makes $G$ constant independently of $u$. Every certificate forcing that value of $H$ must fix at least $r-t$ other representatives. A shorter certificate would make the circuit independent of $u$ in the corresponding cofactor, contrary to the preceding result. The partner is independent of $u$ because $u$ has just one reader and the circuit is acyclic.
