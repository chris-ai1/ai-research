# Related-work audit for the selected Andreev results

Authors: Christian Reitwiessner

Audit date: 2026-11-11. Scope: related work only; this is not a proof certification or submission. Source draft: `threshold_paper/paper.tex`; background: `threshold_paper/novelty_search.md` and `research_backup/docs/RESEARCH_STATE.md`. No shared draft edits.

## Status and method

Completed related-work audit. Primary-paper statements were checked independently. Failed searches are not evidence of novelty. The 2026-11-06 novelty notes describe older versions of several claims and cannot certify priority for the current $5/16$ exact construction, masked mean-square approximation, or restricted lower bounds.

The function under audit is the parity-address lookup $A(x,a)=x_{(|a_1|\bmod 2,\ldots,|a_k|\bmod 2)}$, with $N=2^k$ table bits, $k$ blocks of $m$ address bits, and $n=N+km$. Balanced comparisons use $km=N$ (or $m=\lfloor N/k\rfloor$).

## Claims covered and audit disposition

- **Exact upper:** $(5/16+o(1))n^2$ gates at balance. The clean published comparison is KW Theorem 1.2; credit the specialized construction, not its classical staircase compiler.
- **Approximate uppers:** $O(n^{3/2}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)})$ gates for agreement $1-\delta$, $0<\delta\le1/2$, and $O(\varepsilon^2n^{3/2}\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)})$ gates for agreement $1/2+\varepsilon$, $N^{-1/2}\le\varepsilon\le1/8$. Distinguish masked mean-square analysis from KW's parity windowing and table-majority endpoint.
- **Sign-coherent lower:** $\Omega(n^2/\log^3n)$ gates at balance. The cube/table-entropy combination is the application to explain; IPS supplies the classical monotone-chain ingredient, not this quantitative theorem.
- **Full-support equal-magnitude lower:** at least $2^{m\lfloor k/2\rfloor}$ bottom gates when $m\lfloor k/2\rfloor\ge2$. Present as an elementary consequence of parity order and paired literal substitutions, with no priority claim for the exact corollary.

## Editorial conclusions

- Separate the specialized lookup decomposition and error analysis from classical Fourier expansion, threshold staircases, concentration, and monotone-chain arguments.
- State sign coherence block by block, with signs allowed to differ between gates and blocks; table weights and the output threshold are unrestricted. This is a restricted-model lower bound, not a general $\mathsf{THR}\circ\mathsf{THR}$ lower bound.
- The equal-magnitude hypothesis is exact and includes full support on every address bit for each gate that uses any address bit. It does not mean merely bounded weights, ordinary majority gates with arbitrary support, or comparable magnitudes.
- Do not identify the parity-address lookup with polynomial-containment “Andreev's Problem.”

## Verified findings: exact upper bound and classical components

1. **Use KW's stated upper bound, with its exact location.** Kane–Williams, arXiv:1511.07860v1, Theorem 1.2 (printed p. 2), states a uniform $O(n^3/\log n)$ depth-two LTF upper bound for the ordinary parity-address function. Section 5.2, item 2 (printed p. 13), sketches Fourier expansion into parities and separate symmetric-function implementations. Its $O(n^2)$ bound is for $\mathsf{MOD}_3\circ\mathsf{MOD}_2$, not for depth-two thresholds. Its linear-gate majority construction has depth three. The defensible comparison is “improves the $O(n^3/\log n)$ upper bound stated by Kane and Williams,” rather than an exhaustive “previously best known” claim. [Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07860).

2. **Correct the staircase citation number.** In the 1993 JCSS version of Hajnal–Maass–Pudlák–Szegedy–Turán, the relevant result is **Proposition 2.1, p. 132**, not “Proposition 1.” It realizes any symmetric function by a linear-size depth-two threshold circuit with unit-magnitude weights; the proof's top sum has two adjacent values. Lemma 2.4 and Theorem 2.3 (p. 133) give the related layer-merging simulation. KW calls it “Proposition 1,” but the paper being cited uses 2.1. An arbitrary real-valued function of a bounded integer linear form is the elementary finite-difference extension, not literally the proposition's statement. [Author-hosted primary PDF](https://igi-web.tugraz.at/people/maass/psfiles/47.pdf).

3. **Hansen–Podolskii is a representation theorem, not the specialized upper bound.** ECCC TR13-021, Theorem 5 (printed p. 7), identifies polynomial-length, polynomial-degree PTFs on $\{1,2\}^n$ with polynomial-size $\mathsf{THR}\circ\mathsf{MAJ}$. Its converse uses an exact-threshold indicator for each possible value of each bounded integer linear form. This is good background for the staircase viewpoint, but gives no Andreev decomposition or sharp $5/16$ count. **Lemma 6 on the same page** also converts polynomial-size $\mathsf{THR}\circ\mathsf{MAJ}$ circuits to polynomial-size circuits with monotone bottom gates. The overhead is polynomial, not size-preserving, so it does not transfer the sign-coherent quadratic lower bound to unrestricted circuits at the same exponent. Avoid claiming a separation in polynomial computability from sign coherence. [Primary PDF](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2013/021/download/).

The exact construction should be credited for its particular grouped Walsh score, parity-removal identity, shared corrections and exceptional-address margins. Classical Fourier inversion or threshold staircases alone are not new. The current coefficient $5/16$ is absent from the older project novelty note; do not treat that note's searches for the earlier $3/4$ coefficient as a verified priority audit for the current result.

## Verified findings: approximation and sign-coherent lower bound

**Approximation.** KW Theorem 1.4 and its proof in Section 4 (printed p. 10) already truncate the Hamming-weight staircase for parity to an $O(\sqrt n)$ central window for 99% agreement. KW Section 6, opening paragraph (printed p. 14), also notes the $\Omega(n^{-1/2})$ correlation of ordinary Andreev with majority of its table bits. Thus neither windowing nor the one-gate small-advantage endpoint should be advertised as new. The selected draft's additional contribution is the independent per-character masking identity that cancels *cross-error second moments*, followed by one fixed-mask choice. It is not simply Hoeffding plus a union bound: that pays a $\sqrt{\log n}$ factor at constant error. The tuned $\varepsilon^2$ upper bound uses a subcube vote and this error estimate. Preserve its stated $N^{-1/2}\le\varepsilon\le1/8$ range and nonuniform existence qualification. [KW primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07860).

**Sign-coherent lower bound.** Impagliazzo–Paturi–Saks, Lemma 2.1 (p. 696), gives the classical single-transition property for an oriented monotone chain. Their Corollary 3 (p. 698) gives $(n/2)^{1/(2(d-1))}$ gates for parity in depth $d$ with generalized monotone gates (unate gates), not a near-quadratic bound for the present block-coherent lookup model. Here the quantitative work is the combination of table entropy on *each* unit block-count cube and the $m^k-(m-1)^k$ incidence bound. Ordinary unateness of LTFs is not enough: coherence ensures monotonicity while an entire block moves through its fixed prefix order. The orientations may differ between gates. Cite IPS for the chain ingredient, not for the complete theorem. [Author-hosted primary PDF](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/ImpagliazzoPaturiSaks_1997_siamjc.pdf).

Recommended novelty posture: describe the grouped exact construction, masked second-moment argument, and cube/entropy application explicitly as what this paper proves. The checked primary statements do not furnish these specialized claims, but this audit does not certify firstness. Avoid “no prior bounds exist” and “first quadratic” unless separately substantiated.

## Function and model distinctions that must survive condensation

- **KKL:** ECCC TR17-026, Definition 6.2 and Theorem 6.3, printed p. 32 (PDF p. 34), use $4u$ table bits and $u$ address bits. Degree-$d$ PTF circuits need $u^{1/2+1/d}(\log u)^{-O(d^2)}$ gates and $u^{3/2+1/d}(\log u)^{-O(d^2)}$ wires. At $d=1$ these are the familiar exponents $3/2$ and $5/2$ for a constant-ratio variant of parity-address lookup. KKL explicitly prove the worst-case statement and only remark that a KW-type correlation extension is possible. They do not state the selected upper bounds or the sign-coherent near-quadratic bound. Their generalized/coded depth-three construction is separately Definition 6.7 and Theorem 6.8 (printed p. 34). [Primary PDF](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2017/026/download/).

- **CSS:** Theory of Computing 14(9), Section 2.4, equation (2.1), pp. 11–12, defines $F(x_1,x_2)=\operatorname{Enc}(x_1)_{E(x_2)}$: an encoded table indexed by a bit-fixing extractor. **Both components differ** from the raw table and block parities in ordinary Andreev. Corollary 4.3, p. 17, gives exponentially small correlation against depth-$d$ circuits with $u^{1+\varepsilon_d}$ wires; Theorem 4.13, p. 28, is the stronger intermediate statement. Section 9's Theorem 9.3 (restated p. 47) instead re-proves KW's depth-three result for KW's $B_n$, with $n^{o(1)}$ losses. Neither is an upper bound or an exponentially small-correlation theorem for ordinary parity-address lookup. [Primary journal PDF on arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.06290).

- **Potukuchi 2019:** “Andreev's Problem” asks whether a given $S\subseteq\mathbb F_q\times\mathbb F_q$ contains the graph of a polynomial of degree at most a given $d$. This is related to Reed–Solomon list recovery and $\mathsf{AC}^0[\oplus]$ lower bounds, not the parity-address lookup studied here. Do not cite it as a predecessor for any selected threshold-circuit result. A clarifying footnote is optional; it need not occupy the compact paper's bibliography. [Publisher's primary article record](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2019.25).

## Recent slicing: relevant context, different conclusion

Sauermann–Xu, *Improved lower bound for hypercube edge slicing*, arXiv:2510.16592v1 (2025), Theorem 1.1, p. 1, prove that a collection slicing every edge of the $u$-cube has more than $u^{13/19}/(10^{11}\log^{32/19}u)$ hyperplanes for sufficiently large $u$. Their *Nondegenerate hyperplane covers of the hypercube*, arXiv:2507.00773v2 (2026 revision), Theorem 1.1, p. 2, proves $|\mathcal H|\ge u/2$ for a nondegenerate vertex cover; Corollary 1.2, p. 3, gives $|\mathcal H|\ge u/(4C)$ for edge slicing with normals in $\{-C,\ldots,C\}^u$. The latter explicitly permits zero entries. [Slicing theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16592v1), [bounded-normal corollary](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00773v2).

These are not statements of the selected sign-coherent or exponential Andreev bounds. In the coherent proof a gate is a coordinatewise-monotone function of prefix counts, and need not be a hyperplane in the block-count coordinates when individual within-block magnitudes vary. In the exponential proof the decisive condition is exact cancellation of paired coefficients under literal substitutions, not edge slicing. Applying slicing separately to fixed-table parity restrictions does not by itself multiply the lower bound by the number of table entries: gates can be reused across restrictions. Any such transfer needs its own incidence argument and hypotheses. This explains why the additional table-support-restricted slicing corollaries in `RESEARCH_STATE.md` should not silently be imported into the four selected claims.

## Exponential equal-magnitude lower bound: attribution and novelty assessment

**There is a precise classical citation missing from the draft: Minsky–Papert's parity-order theorem.** In *Perceptrons*, Section 3.1, Theorem 3.1.1 (pp. 56–57), and Corollary 1 on p. 58, parity requires at least one intermediate predicate supported on the entire input set. This is stronger than the fact that parity is not one LTF: it applies to a threshold of *arbitrary* predicates each missing a coordinate. I visually checked the actual scanned pages (PDF pp. 71–73 in the retrieved scan); the book metadata and expanded-edition provenance are confirmed by [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262631112/perceptrons/). The Fourier-orthogonality proof in the draft is a short proof of this classical fact.

The remaining reduction is elementary and should be exposed as such:

1. Fix the lookup table to parity of $r=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ address bits, giving parity on $d=rm$ raw selected bits $z$, while $d$ other bits $w$ are ignored. If needed, fix the remaining odd block.
2. For each $t\in\{-1,1\}^d$, substitute $w_i=t_i z_i$. A gate with paired signs $\sigma_i,\tau_i$ has new coefficient proportional to $\sigma_i+t_i\tau_i$. To retain every selected variable, it needs the unique vector $t_i=\sigma_i\tau_i$ for all $i$.
3. Every one of the $2^d$ resulting parity representations needs a fully dependent predicate by Minsky–Papert; each allowed bottom gate can provide one for at most one vector $t$. Therefore $G\ge2^d$. Raw affine top terms do not supply a full-support predicate when $d\ge2$.

**Assessment:** the displayed exponential bound is a straightforward restricted-class consequence of the classical parity-order obstruction plus literal pairing/covering. I did **not** locate a primary source stating this exact Andreev corollary or establishing priority for the pairing trick. These two facts should not be conflated: it would be unjustified either to call the exact bound a known published theorem or to advertise a new general exponential lower-bound technique. Recommend a short proposition/corollary, attributed at the ingredient level, rather than a headline novelty claim. The draft's existing disclaimer of priority is appropriate; replace its later “The new result” with “This restricted-class bound.”

Suggested sentence: “For completeness, we record an exponential obstruction when every address-dependent bottom gate has full address support and exactly equal coefficient magnitudes. It follows by paired literal substitutions from the classical fact that a threshold representation of parity must contain a predicate depending on every variable [Minsky–Papert, Section 3.1].”

The exact-equality and full-support assumptions are essential to this argument. A zero or unequal paired coefficient can leave a gate dependent for more than one substitution. The selected exact upper bound uses zero supports and therefore does not conflict with this proposition. This is not a lower bound for ordinary majority gates with freely chosen supports, for all bounded-weight threshold gates, or for approximately equal magnitudes. The broader near-equal/two-scale constructions in the research archive make it especially inadvisable to describe this as a robust bounded-weight obstruction.

## Suggested compact related-work wording

The following is intended as roughly half a page, with reference keys mapped below; it deliberately makes no exhaustive priority claim.

> We study the ordinary parity-address version of Andreev's function. Kane and Williams [KW, Theorem 1.2 and Section 5.2] state an $O(n^3/\log n)$ depth-two threshold upper bound. Our exact construction improves this to $(5/16+o(1))n^2$ gates. Threshold staircases for symmetric functions are classical [HMPST, Proposition 2.1]; Hansen and Podolskii [HP, Theorem 5] give a related representation by functions of bounded integer linear forms. The construction here uses a grouped lookup identity and shared corrections to obtain its quadratic count.
>
> Central-window approximations already appear for parity in [KW, Theorem 1.4]. Our approximation argument uses independent address masks to cancel cross-character error moments, giving $O(n^{3/2}\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)})$ gates for uniform-input error $\delta$. Combining this estimate with a subcube majority vote gives the stated $\varepsilon^2$ size dependence. These are deterministic nonuniform circuits after the masks are fixed. The generalized Andreev function used for strong correlation bounds in [CSS, Section 2.4 and Corollary 4.3] uses an encoded table and an extractor and is a different function. Kabanets, Kane and Lu [KKL, Theorem 6.3] extend the restriction-based lower-bound approach to polynomial threshold gates.
>
> Our near-quadratic lower bound assumes that each bottom gate's coefficients have a common sign within each address block. Its single-transition ingredient is classical [IPS, Lemma 2.1]; the proof combines it with table entropy over all unit block-count cubes. Separately, we record an elementary exponential bound for full-support, exactly equal-magnitude address coefficients. This follows from paired literal substitutions and the classical parity-order obstruction [MP, Section 3.1]. Neither restriction is imposed on general depth-two threshold circuits.

If the geometric context is discussed, one additional sentence suffices: “Recent hypercube slicing and covering bounds [SXslice, SXcover] concern different incidence conditions and do not directly yield the block-coherent or paired-cancellation bounds proved here.” Potukuchi's unrelated problem need not be mentioned unless the name causes confusion.

## Search coverage and residual limitations

Primary statements were checked for KW, HMPST, HP, IPS, KKL, CSS, the two Sauermann–Xu papers, Potukuchi, and the Minsky–Papert parity-order theorem. Searches additionally covered combinations of ordinary Andreev, quadratic threshold upper bounds, masked approximation, sign coherence, partial parity, full support, equal weights and equal magnitudes. Broad queries also returned unrelated physics and neural-learning material; these were not used as evidence. Negative search results establish no priority.

A current-date check of [Chen–Tal–Wang, ECCC TR26-039](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/039/) and [Nag, ECCC TR26-167](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/167/) confirms that their advertised stronger exponents concern languages in $\mathsf E^{\mathsf{NP}}$, not the selected ordinary function. Only their abstracts/records were independently inspected in this audit, so they should not support a detailed technical comparison here. The compact paper need not reproduce the original broad state-of-the-art table.

This report audits attribution and comparisons; it does not certify every algebraic identity, sharp coefficient, or finite-parameter range in the draft. The principal remaining priority uncertainty is whether some earlier source already explicitly formulated the literal-pairing corollary. Its elementary derivation is clear regardless of that historical question.
